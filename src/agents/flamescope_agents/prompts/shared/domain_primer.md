DOMAIN PRIMER (glossary for NASA combustion science; not a source of facts about any investigation)

- Microgravity (0 g) versus normal gravity (1 g): in microgravity there is no buoyancy, so hot gas does not rise and flames can be spherical, weaker and slower; normal gravity drives buoyant flow. Partial gravity (for example lunar or Martian) lies between; very little flame data exists there. That is the partial-gravity gap.
- Buoyancy: upward flow of hot gas caused by gravity. Its absence changes flame shape, spread rate and extinction.
- Opposed flow: air flows against the direction the flame spreads. Concurrent flow: air flows in the direction of spread. Quiescent: no imposed flow. Coflow: fuel and oxidizer streams flow in the same direction.
- Flammability limit: the lowest oxygen fraction, or the condition, below which a flame cannot sustain itself. Extinction: the flame going out.
- Oxygen fraction (share of the gas that is O2) is not oxygen partial pressure (oxygen fraction times total pressure). Both matter for flammability.
- Cool flames: low-temperature, faintly luminous combustion that can persist after the visible flame is gone.
- Hardware: CIR (Combustion Integrated Rack), MSG (Microgravity Science Glovebox), MDCA (Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus), ACME (Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments), the SPICE duct. Hosts such as CIR and MSG carry apparatus; instruments measure.
- Fire-safety relevance: ignition, flame spread, flammability limits, extinction, suppression, smoke and aerosol detection, soot, and post-fire hazards in crewed spacecraft and habitats.
